## Introduction 
### Protecting Hawaiʻi
Kāhili is is an application designed to foster sustainable tourism in Hawaiʻi by providing cultural insights and actions for conservation, empowering tourists to actively contribute to the preservation of Hawaiʻi's natural and cultural heritage. Hawaiʻi is home to one of the most unique and sacred ecosystems in the world. The land (ʻāina) and ocean (kai) are deeply interconnected with the Native Hawaiian identity and spirituality. The biodiversity of Hawaiʻi is significant environmentally but also is the foundation of tradition and culture for generations. The Hawaiian Islands are in danger, and action must be taken now. Nearly 50% of Hawaiʻi’s coral reefs have been lost or severely degraded (NOAA, 2020), more than 90% of native ecosystems have been altered (Hawaiʻi DLNR), and Hawaiʻi is known is the “endangered species capital of the world” with over 500 federally listed threatened or endangered species (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service). Action needs to be taken now to protect Hawaiʻi, and it starts with us.

### Destructive Tourism
Although a major component of Hawaiʻi’s economy, tourism is a strain on both the natural environment and local culture. Prior to COVID-19, Hawaiʻi saw over 10 million visitors annually, which far exceeds its resident population (Hawaiʻi Tourism Authority, 2019). This level of visitation has led to overcrowding, reef damage, tidal erosion, depletion of resources and overconsumption, and the commodification and misrepresentation of Native Hawaiian culture. Without proper education and regulation, tourism can inadvertently shift from appreciation to exploitation, placing additional pressure on already fragile ecosystems and culturally significant spaces. Kāhili was created with the intent to provide simple and accessible actions for visitors to take which are culturally and environmentally influenced, promoting the preservation of Hawaiʻi’s land and culture.

### Our Brand, Our Progress
Kāhili is an ongoing initiative that was established in Fall 2023. Influenced by the book, “50 Small Things You Can Do To Save Hawaiʻi” by Chaminade University of Honolulu’s Dr. Gail Grabowsky, Kāhili was a vision brought to life through the student internship program of ‘Inana. Kāhili and its concept of a virtual and accessible resource to guide visitors towards environmental sustainability and cultural preservation is one that was developed for 2.5 years before the conclusion of funding for the ‘Inana program. The mission behind Kāhili has remained, thus I was compelled to finally build out our idea. Since its creation, the project has evolved through research, design iteration, and community-centered thinking, shaping the foundation of a platform to evoke change, which is now a physical resource. Kāhili is a traditional feather standard created for Ali‘i and/or family of the Hawaiian Islands to represent mana. Standing tall and created using thousands of feathers in small amounts from many birds of Hawaiʻi, Kāhili is symbolic of awareness and sustainability.

## Where We Are and What's Next
### Where We Are
Until this project, Kāhili was merely a concept – but one that was deeply and intricately thought out with environmental and cultural considerations. The primary goal of this capstone project was to bring Kāhili to life with an active web application, while also providing content and design improvements. Using the cultural and environmental information gathered through the ‘Inana cohorts, the web application was created using html and css code and hosted on hosted on a Flask framework in Python. The resulting application features a fully navigable multi-page website with six category pages — Land and Sea, Wildlife and Sealife, Hawaiʻi Lifestyle, Community, Visiting Hawaiʻi, and Laws. Each category page directs users to real organizations and volunteer opportunities across the Hawaiian islands, bridging the gap between awareness and action and fostering sustainable and culturally respectful tourism.

### What's Next
There are many plans in place for the advancement of Kāhili. First, the logos on the homepage are to be replaced with the artwork of local artists. Also, the application is to open with a Google Maps API feature where users input their address and the nearest sustainable travel locations populate with directions. Importantly, the application is to have user accounts and a database where after each activity completed, the user gains a ”feather” to add to their personal “Kāhili”, creating an interactive and incentivized experience. I would like to partner with local sustainable organizations to where if we direct users to them, the organization could provide discounts to users. Content and UI will continually develop and be enhanced.
