LAND AND SEA

Native Hawaiians have a deep historical connection to both land and sea. Their ancestors arrived in the Hawaiian Islands around 1,500 years ago, bringing advanced knowledge of navigation and agriculture. The land was seen as sacred, with each family and community stewarding specific areas, practicing sustainable farming and fishing. The sea played an equally vital role, providing resources and serving as a pathway for exploration. Native Hawaiians developed a system of land divisions (ahupuaʻa) that connected the mountains to the ocean, ensuring balance and respect for nature. In Hawaiian mythology, the land and sea are personified as deities, such as Pele, the goddess of fire and volcanoes, and Kanaloa, the god of the sea. These divine beings represent the interconnectedness of nature. The Hawaiian belief system emphasizes respect and stewardship for both land and sea, as they are seen as living, sacred entities that nourish and protect the people. By caring for the environment, Native Hawaiians honor these deities, ensuring the balance of nature and the sustainability of resources for future generations.
