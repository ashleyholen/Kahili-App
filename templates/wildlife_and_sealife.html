WILDLIFE AND SEALIFE

Native Hawaiians have long held a deep respect for wildlife and sealife, recognizing them as vital parts of the ecosystem. Ancient Hawaiians practiced sustainable hunting, fishing, and farming, ensuring a harmonious relationship with nature. Species like the Hawaiian monk seal, honu (sea turtle), and various fish were revered, and traditional practices, such as kapu (taboos), protected them from overharvesting. The Polynesian ancestors brought knowledge of fishing techniques and animal management, while Hawaiian cultural practices emphasized the spiritual connection between humans, wildlife, and the sea, promoting respect and sustainability.
